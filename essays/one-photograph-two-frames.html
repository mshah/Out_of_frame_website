Photographic case note · Cooch Behar, c. 1896

One photograph.
Two frames.

01 / A shared negative, different printed views

Two crops.
Two emphases.

British hunter Peter Burges took a photograph of a dead rhinoceros surrounded by elephants and members of a hunting party in Assam. In 1898, an illustrated account of his hunting experiences in McClure’s Magazine printed the scene with the riders’ heads cut off. Two years later, Wild Sports of Burma and Assam printed a different portion of the same exposure.

The later edition does not simply restore everything missing from the earlier one. It extends upwards to reveal more people and cuts away more of the animal below. There is no single complete view among these two surviving publications.

The comparison does not tell us that either publisher intended to conceal a particular person. It demonstrates how editorial boundaries can alter the balance between the people and the animal within an otherwise authentic photograph.

02 / The words beside the photograph

Two captions.
Two accounts.

1898 / McClure’s“Elephants gathering round a rhinoceros that has just been shot from the howdah.”
1900 / Pollok & Thom“A Dead Rhinoceros.”

The first caption stages an action and identifies where the shot was fired. The later caption emphasises the result. The framing changes visually, while the wording narrows from an event to a trophy. Neither the image nor the caption contains the full history of the animal or the labour of the expedition.

03 / Evidence & limits

What the frame
cannot settle.

Historian L. C. Rookmaaker links Burges’s photograph to a Cooch Behar hunting expedition associated with Maharaja Nripendra Narayan in 1896, but the exact rhinoceros and exposure date remain unidentified. The people in these reproductions cannot be named confidently from the images alone.

We do not know who chose either publication crop, why, or how much more of the scene the original negative recorded. The digital comparison uses modern extracts of two historical prints; it is not a digitally recovered full photograph.

Visibility is not the same as recognition. A person may re-enter a published frame without their name, contribution or experience re-entering the record.
